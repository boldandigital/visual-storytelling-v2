## PART I — THE SITE STORY (visitor's journey)

The site tells the story of **one person opening one door**. From the
moment they land, they are being shown a different way of working.
By the time they hit the contact section, they don't need to be
sold — they've already decided.

**The seven acts, in narrative order:**

### ACT 0 — ARRIVAL: "What is this?"

The visitor lands. They see a single navy ceramic bowl on a
seamless white stage. The cyan ring inside the rim is the only
colour. Three small objects orbit slowly: a clapperboard, a plant,
a paper plane. For the first 1.5 seconds, **that is all they see.**
No type. No nav. No text. Just the bowl, in motion, on a stage.

Why: the visitor needs to **stop scrolling** before we can talk to
them. The bowl is the bait. It works because a 3D object rotating
in a clean stage is **rare** on the modern web — almost every other
site is a 2D grid of text and rectangles. The bowl arrests.

At 1.5s, the type begins to assemble. At 4.0s, the visitor has
absorbed: (1) this is a 2026 agency site, (2) the studio is
technically serious, (3) the cyan ring is the brand, (4) the bowl
is the studio's mascot / identity object.

The visitor exits this act with: **curiosity, and a slight
adrenaline spike.** They didn't expect a 3D object. They are now
leaning forward.

### ACT 1 — PROOF: "They can build things."

The visitor scrolls once. The bowl video freezes (the scroll pauses
the orbit). The marquee band slides up from the bottom edge of
the screen, full-bleed navy, cyan borders, single line of text
scrolling left at 38s per loop: "STRATEGY · CODE · 3D · AI ·
HOSTING · BRAND WORLDS ·" — the studio's vocabulary, looping
endlessly.

Why: the marquee is the **anti-scroll**. After the bowl's
cinematic stillness, the marquee is loud, fast, public. It says:
"we don't just sit and look. We move. We make. We ship." It's a
proof of motion-design capability without showing any work.

The visitor exits this act with: **the recognition that the
marquee itself is a piece of design.** They start trusting the
studio.

### ACT 2 — CAPABILITIES: "We can do what we need."

The marquee ends. A clean white stage returns. The visitor now
sees **five services**, each presented in its own full-viewport
moment. They scroll past each one, slowly, reading the headline
and the lead. The rhythm alternates: service 1 has the asset on
the right, service 2 has it on the left, etc. The eye learns the
rhythm after the first two.

Each service is a 4-second reading: eyebrow, asset, headline,
lead, "→ View case" link. The link does nothing (or scrolls to
Work) — but the visitor is being told "this is one of five things
we do, and we've done it before."

The visitor exits this act with: **a list of capabilities, ranked
by visual weight, ready to cite in their next meeting.** They
can now defend the choice of hiring this studio.

### ACT 3 — METHOD: "They'll deliver."

The five services end. A single horizontal navy line draws itself
across the viewport in 1.2 seconds. Four nodes appear on the line:
01 BRIEF / 02 STRATEGY / 03 BUILD / 04 OPERATE. Each node has a
title and a one-line description. The line is dotted from 03 to
04 to indicate ongoing retainer.

Why: the visitor has just been sold the **what** (capabilities).
Now they need the **how** (process). The horizontal line is the
simplest possible way to communicate a sequence: left to right,
past tense, done. No branching, no decision trees.

The visitor exits this act with: **trust.** They believe the
studio will not disappear mid-project. They know what they're
signing up for.

### ACT 4 — WORK: "They've done it before."

The method line ends. The screen fades to off-white. Three rows
appear, each one a typographic statement of a past client:
"LiteSpeed" / "QUIC.cloud" / "aaads". On hover, the row's
background flips to a still from the project, and the text
inverts via `mix-blend-mode: difference` (cyan to navy, white
to off-white). The arrow translates +6px.

Why: the visitor needs **social proof** to convert. The list
format ("3 client names, big type") is the **2014 way** to do
this. The 2026 way is: name the client, don't show the work, let
the typographic confidence be the proof. If the studio is good
enough to write their own name in 96px Inter, they're good
enough to handle the visitor's brand.

The visitor exits this act with: **recognition.** Either they
recognize the client names and feel safe, or they don't recognize
them and trust the studio's confidence to be enough.

### ACT 5 — MANIFESTO: "I'd work with these people."

The work list ends. The screen goes navy. One sentence, centered,
in off-white Inter 56px: "Three-dimensional web. AI automation.
Razor-sharp brand strategy. One shipping crew." Above it, a
1px cyan horizontal line, 40px wide, scales from 0% to 100% in
800ms. Below it, a small mono line in cyan: "— Bold And Digital
· 2026."

Why: this is the **breath**. The visitor has been reading,
processing, scrolling. Now they stop. One sentence. Six seconds.
No motion, no buttons, no links. The visitor either resonates
with the sentence or doesn't. If they do, they become a fan. If
they don't, they leave — and that's fine, they were never the
audience.

The visitor exits this act with: **alignment.** They've decided
if they're the right fit for the studio. The studio isn't for
everyone. The sentence makes that clear.

### ACT 6 — DOORS: "One email. Done."

The manifesto fades. The screen returns to off-white. The eyebrow
"— CONTACT / 06" appears. A massive H2: "Initialize\na project."
in Inter 140px. A single lead: "One email. We reply within one
business day." A single button: "contact@boldandigital.com →"
in navy, off-white text, pill shape. Below, three meta items:
STUDIO / HOURS / REPLY in mono.

Why: this is the **relief**. The visitor has been on a journey.
The payoff is the smallest possible next step. **One email.
That's it.** No form. No dropdown. No "let's schedule a call."
The friction is a single keystroke after they hit the button.

The visitor exits this act with: **momentum.** They've made the
decision. The studio has earned the click.

### ACT 7 — ECHO: "Bold and digital."

The contact section ends. A second marquee band, navy, cyan border,
slides in. Same text as ACT 1 but in REVERSE direction (right to
left), slower (60s per loop), all in cyan: "BOLD AND DIGITAL ·
BOLD AND DIGITAL · BOLD AND DIGITAL ·" repeated.

Why: the visitor has reached the bottom. The first thing they
saw was the bowl, then the marquee. The last thing they see is
the marquee, slower, reversed, in brand colour. **The studio's
name is the last word they read before they leave.** The echo
is deliberate. The visitor closes the tab, but the name is
still in their peripheral vision.

The visitor exits the site with: **the name, the cyan, the
ring.** They will remember this site longer than any other agency
site they visited today. That's the win.

## PART II — THE HERO STORY (animation, frame by frame)

The hero is **8 seconds of cinema, then 1 minute of living room.**
The first 8 seconds tell the entire story of the studio without a
single word. The next 60 seconds are the visitor sitting in the
living room, looking at the bowl, scrolling when they're ready.

### T = 0.0s — Black

The visitor's browser paints the first frame. The screen is
**pure off-white** (`#F7F8F9`). No flash of unstyled content. The
nav, the wordmark, the type — all invisible.

The bowl video element exists in the DOM but `opacity: 0`. The
poster image (`hero-bowl-orbital-poster.jpg`) is loaded but
hidden.

**Why:** the visitor needs to feel **arrival** before they can
feel curiosity. A blank off-white canvas is a stage. It's not a
page. It's a room.

### T = 0.4s — Bowl appears

A navy ceramic bowl fades in at the centre of the right 60% of
the viewport. **Scale: 0.3 → 0.85 over 800ms with
`cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`.** Opacity: 0 → 1 over 800ms
linear. The cyan ring inside the rim lights up at T = 0.8s
(opacity 0 → 0.9, no scale change, 400ms ease-out).

The bowl is a still frame from the orbital video (the poster).
It is not yet rotating. **It is sitting on a stage, posing.**

**Why:** the bowl needs to **land** before it moves. A bowl that
moves as it fades in feels like a stock animation. A bowl that
lands and then moves feels alive.

### T = 1.2s — Bowl begins to orbit

The video starts playing. The bowl's orbital camera move begins:
a 360° rotation around the bowl over 24 seconds. The three
objects (clapperboard, plant, paper plane) begin drifting
clockwise around the bowl, each at a slightly different speed
(0.04 rad/s, 0.06 rad/s, 0.05 rad/s) so they pass each other
instead of locking in formation.

**Why:** the bowl is now **alive**. The visitor has watched it
land. Now it moves. The objects orbiting at different speeds
are a subtle visual proof of physics — they're not on a fixed
track, they're floating in 3D space.

### T = 1.5s — Wordmark appears

The BOLD/DIGITAL wordmark fades in at the top-left, 11px mono
caps, navy, 600ms ease-out. The cyan dot in the bottom-right
corner of the wordmark (per BRAND.md) pulses once.

**Why:** the wordmark is the first **identity** signal. The
visitor now knows: this is a brand. Not a portfolio. Not a
template. A brand.

### T = 2.0s — Nav appears

The thin top-right nav fades in: /SERVICES / WORK / PROCESS
/START →. The /START pill has a 1.2px cyan border. 600ms
ease-out, 200ms after the wordmark.

**Why:** the nav is the **permission structure**. The visitor
now knows they can navigate. But they don't need to — the
marquee will tell them when to scroll.

### T = 2.4s — Eyebrow types itself

The eyebrow line `./initialize_studio --brand=boldandigital`
types itself out character by character, 25ms per character,
total duration 800ms. It starts as a single empty cursor
(blinking, 1.2s period). The text is 11px mono, cyan, on the
left 40% of the viewport.

**Why:** the typing animation is **programming-language** in
feel. It says: "we are a studio that writes code." A static
eyebrow would say: "we are a marketing site." The cursor blink
is the visitor's anchor — the eye lands on the cursor first,
then the text reveals.

### T = 3.2s — H1 lands

The H1 "Brand worlds." lands on the left 40% of the viewport.
**It does not fade in — it scales up from 0.92 to 1.00 over
600ms** with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`. The opacity goes
0 → 1 over 400ms in parallel. The H1 is 96px Inter 700, navy,
with a cyan period at the end.

**Why:** scale-in feels more **physical** than fade-in. The
visitor perceives the H1 as something that **arrived**, not
something that appeared. The cyan period is the **only** cyan
character in the H1 — it draws the eye to the end of the line
and signals: "this is the end of the thought, but not the end
of the story."

### T = 3.8s — Lead fades in

The lead paragraph fades in below the H1: 14px JetBrains Mono,
navy, 60ch max width, 600ms ease-out. The text is:

> A design and engineering studio for brands shaping tomorrow.
> Three-dimensional web. AI automation. Razor-sharp brand
> strategy. One shipping crew.

**Why:** the lead is the **first complete sentence** the visitor
reads. It must be short, declarative, and contain the studio's
four pillars (3D web, AI, brand, one crew). The mono typeface
says: "this is technical copy, not marketing copy."

### T = 4.4s — CTAs slide up

Two buttons slide up from 20px below their final position,
400ms ease-out: "Initialize Project →" (navy filled, off-white
text) and "See the work" (transparent, navy border, navy text).
The slide-up direction is from below — the visitor perceives the
buttons as **rising into place**, not appearing.

**Why:** slide-up from below is the universal "action available"
gesture. The visitor's eye is now in the bottom half of the
text column. The buttons land where the eye is.

### T = 5.0s — Service tags fade in

A small line of mono text fades in below the CTAs: "SERVICES ·
brand · websites · imagery · ai · hosting". 11px, dim, 600ms
ease-out.

**Why:** the service tags are the **proof of breadth**. The
visitor now knows the studio has at least 5 services. They're
not just a 3D shop, not just an AI shop, not just a brand shop.

### T = 5.6s — Scroll cue appears

A small "SCROLL ↓" cue appears at the bottom-centre of the
viewport. A 1px vertical line, 36px tall, gradient from cyan to
transparent, pulses (opacity 0.3 → 1 → 0.3) over 1.8s infinite.

**Why:** the scroll cue is the **hand-off**. The bowl has
finished its opening act. The visitor knows what they're looking
at. Now they need permission to continue. The cue says: "yes,
scroll. There's more."

### T = 6.0s → ∞ — Living room

The visitor has 1.5 seconds of complete stillness: bowl
rotating, eyebrow dot pulsing, scroll cue pulsing. The H1 is
fully visible. The lead is readable. The buttons are clickable.

**At this point, the visitor either scrolls or doesn't.**

**If they don't scroll** (e.g. they came here from a link and
want to read the page first), the bowl keeps rotating. The H1
stays the same. Nothing else happens. The page is calm and
readable. The visitor can sit here for 30 seconds and nothing
will chase them.

**If they scroll**, the bowl's orbital camera move **does NOT
pause**. The bowl keeps rotating. The text in the left column
cross-fades to the next H1 ("Strategy + code.") at scroll
position 0.3 of the viewport, and to the third H1 ("Ship it.")
at scroll position 0.7. The cross-fade is 700ms with an 8px Y
shift. The cyan period stays in place — it doesn't move
between H1s.

**Why scroll-keyed, not time-keyed:** the visitor's scroll
**is the story**. If the H1 cycled every 2.8s on a setInterval,
the visitor would feel **chased**. With scroll-keying, the
visitor feels **in control**. The story progresses at the
visitor's pace. The bowl is the only constant — it rotates at
its own pace, indifferent to the visitor, which makes it feel
**alive** rather than **responsive**.

## PART III — THE HERO RULES (for the engineer)

These rules are binding. If a rule is broken, the hero is no
longer 2026.

1. **The bowl rotates always.** 24-second period, 360° orbital.
   The rotation is the heartbeat of the site. It does not pause
   on scroll, on hover, on focus. It rotates forever.

2. **The H1 changes only on scroll.** Three states:
   - "Brand worlds." (default, first paint)
   - "Strategy + code." (scroll position 0.3 of viewport)
   - "Ship it." (scroll position 0.7 of viewport)
   No setInterval. No auto-advance. The visitor controls.

3. **Cyan is used exactly four times in the hero.** The period
   at the end of the H1, the eyebrow text, the /START nav pill,
   the cyan dot in the wordmark. Anything else cyan is too much.

4. **The bowl is on the right 60%.** Never centred, never
   left-aligned. The text is on the left 40%. The eye lands
   text-first, then drifts right to the bowl.

5. **The H1 is 96–140px on desktop, 56px on mobile.** Sentence
   case. Inter 700. Letter-spacing -0.04em. No italic. No
   `mix-blend-mode: difference` (that's for the Work section).
   The H1 in the hero is **pure type** — no tricks, no
   cleverness, just size and weight.

6. **The lead is mono, 14px, 60ch max width.** It does not
   change. It does not animate. It is read, not watched.

7. **The bowl video has NO on-screen text.** No "$299 — Buy
   Now" overlay. No "Ready, Steady, Record." Those were the
   LOOP recorder's elements. Ours is the bowl, alone. The
   studio is the product, not the bowl.

8. **The vertical "BOWL-01 / 3D ASSET / LOOP" fine print is
   always visible, always at -90° rotation, always on the
   right edge of the right 60%.** It is the only text on the
   right side of the hero. It is the Mantis-style fine print.

9. **The "FROM $4,800 USD" price tag is always visible, always
   in the bottom-right of the right 60%.** It is the LOOP
   pattern. It says: "we charge real money, and we're not
   ashamed of the number."

10. **There is no auto-play sound.** The hero video is muted by
    default. Sound is a 2024 pattern that aged badly. The hero
    is silent.

## PART IV — THE SITE RULES (cross-cutting)

These rules apply to the whole site, not just the hero.

### Composition
- **60/40 splits** are the default. 50/50 is for the manifesto.
  70/30 is for the contact.
- **Asymmetric grid.** Nothing is ever perfectly centred unless
  the moment is the manifesto.
- **One focal point per viewport.** The eye lands in < 400ms.
- **Negative space ≥ 40%** of every viewport.

### Typography
- **Display:** Inter Variable 400/500/700. H1 = 96–140px
  desktop, 56px mobile. Sentence case. Tight letter-spacing
  (-0.04em).
- **Mono:** JetBrains Mono for eyebrows, meta, prices, fine
  print. NEVER for H1/H2.
- **Eyebrows:** 11px, all caps, letter-spacing 0.32em, cyan.
- **Copy rhythm:** Display → Eyebrow → Display → Mono detail →
  Mono CTA. Never three display blocks in a row.

### Color
- **Stage:** Off-white `#F7F8F9` for content sections. Pure
  white `#FFFFFF` for product moments (hero, capabilities).
  Navy `#051E40` for manifesto + contact.
- **Brand:** Navy + cyan only. Cyan ≤ 5% of viewport area.
- **Text:** Navy on white. Off-white on navy. Never navy on
  navy. Never white on white. Never cyan as a background.

### Motion
- **All easing is `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`.** Never
  `linear`, never `ease-in-out` for content. (Loaders are the
  only exception.)
- **Duration:** 600–900ms for entry, 1200–1800ms for exit.
- **CSS-first.** Only Three.js for the bowl itself. Everything
  else is `transform` + `opacity`.
- **No parallax on text.** Parallax on backgrounds only.
